Camino Primitivo

The original route. Mountainous, wild and deeply rewarding.

The Primitivo is the oldest Camino, the road King Alfonso II walked from Oviedo in the ninth century. It cuts through the mountains of Asturias and Galicia, meets the Francés at Melide and shares the last days in towards Santiago.

It is short, steep and spectacular. There are fewer services and bigger climbs than on the Francés, and that is precisely why experienced walkers have it as their favourite.

History and character

The Camino Primitivo is also called the Original Way, because it is the oldest of all the routes. It winds through the heart of northern Spain from Oviedo to Santiago, the road King Alfonso II already walked in the ninth century, and it appeals to adventurous souls who are looking for a more challenging and less travelled way.

It is a route where you explore both the beautiful landscapes of northern Spain and the depths of your own inner landscape. Walking here is as much a mental as a physical challenge, and many people use it for immersion, quiet and reflection. In return it rewards you with an authentic pilgrim experience far from the crowds.

You can easily combine the Primitivo with both the Francés and the del Norte, since all three lie in northern Spain and lead towards the same goal. The route meets the Francés at Melide and shares the last days in towards Santiago.

Highlights along the way

1

Oviedo

An elegant start town with an old, magnificent cathedral and pre-Romanesque churches on the mountainside. The very place where the pilgrim tradition began.

2

The mountains and villages of Asturias

Green valleys and mountain passes past small stops like Grado, Salas, Tineo and Pola de Allande. Silence and wide open spaces from early morning.

3

The Hospitales route

The high route along the ridge past the ruins of medieval pilgrim hospitals. The route's most dramatic and photographed stage.

4

Grandas de Salime

The descent to the great reservoir is one of the route's big moments, with water and mountains as far as the eye can see.

5

Lugo

The town with a completely preserved Roman wall that you can walk all the way around, and an old Roman bridge. World Heritage.

6

Melide and the meeting with the Francés

Here the Primitivo joins the great route, and Melide is famous for its pulpo, octopus the Galician way.

How hard is the Camino Primitivo?

The Primitivo is considered the hardest Camino. It is short in kilometres, but almost a constant run of mountain passes, forest paths and country roads, with several days of around 25 to 27 kilometres in demanding terrain. The high point for many is the Hospitales route across the mountains, spectacular in clear weather and exposed in mist and rain, where the low route's villages are the safe alternative. If you are in good shape and used to walking, you do not necessarily have to train much beforehand. But we ourselves are used neither to carrying extra weight nor to mountain terrain in Denmark, so a little fitness and strength in the legs and back make the long days nicer. There are fewer services than on the Francés, so plan your stages and your water accordingly.

Who does the Camino Primitivo suit?

The Primitivo is the experienced pilgrim's favourite. It suits you if you like mountains and hard terrain, and are happy to swap comfort and company for wildness and silence. If you travel on a budget and sleep in hostels, a day can cost around 20 to 30 euros, and along the way you can even save a little on the food budget. We found chestnuts by the road, for example. If you want to test your physical limits and at the same time make room for reflection, this is the route.

How do you get to the start of the Camino Primitivo?

The start town of Oviedo is the capital of Asturias and is reached by flying to Asturias airport or by train and bus from Madrid, Bilbao or León. From Denmark the simplest way often goes via Madrid or Bilbao and onward on the Spanish rail network. Take a day in Oviedo first, see the old cathedral, collect your pilgrim passport, and walk out of the city on the original way.

How long is the Camino Primitivo?

The Camino Primitivo is about 320 km from Oviedo to Santiago de Compostela. The sources range from 306 to 323 km, depending on where they measure from and to, and which variants they count.

Why do the numbers vary?
The route has variants
In several places you can choose between two ways, and they are not the same length. On the Camino Primitivo, Gronze lists the stage from Borres to Berducedo in two versions: 24.1 km via Hospitales and 28.6 km via Pola de Allande. Which way a source counts moves the total by several kilometres. Source: Gronze.
The end point is not the same
The sources do not agree on where the route ends. The Spanish federation of Camino associations puts the town of Fisterra 86.14 km from Santiago but gives 89.4 km for the main route, because it continues out to the cape. The three kilometres or so from the town to the lighthouse explain most of the difference between the low and the high figures for the Camino Finisterre. Source: Federación Española de Asociaciones de Amigos del Camino de Santiago.
The headline figure counts more than one stretch
A figure at the top of a page does not always cover the route you plan to walk. Eroski Consumer gives 939.7 km and 37 stages for the Camino Francés. That is the 31 stages from Saint Jean Pied de Port (775.3 km) added to the six stages from Somport (164.4 km), which is another start of the same route. Source: Eroski Consumer.
The path gets re-routed
The routes are not fixed for all time. Gronze writes that the Xunta de Galicia changed the course of the Camino Inglés between Betanzos and Hospital de Bruma in 2017, and that the stretch became shorter and less steep. A source that has not been measured again after a re-routing therefore shows a different figure from a newer one. Source: Gronze.
Measurement and rounding
Even a single source can have two figures for the same stretch. The Spanish federation gives 112.47 km for the main route from Ferrol next to its GPS track download and calls the figures approximate, while its town list on the same page puts Ferrol 114.98 km from Santiago. The Xunta de Galicia gives 112.4 km in its info box and 112.5 km in the text. When stages are rounded and added up, the sum shifts a little each time. Source: Federación Española de Asociaciones de Amigos del Camino de Santiago.

The stages further down follow Gronze's division, which is why they add up to Gronze's figure.

The route on the map

When should you walk the Camino Primitivo?

Late May to early October. The mountain stages are genuinely exposed, so if you walk outside high season, keep an eye on the forecast, especially around the Hospitales variant.

Frequently asked questions about the Camino Primitivo

How long is the Camino Primitivo, and how long does it take?
The route is around 320 kilometres and takes 12 to 14 days over 14 stages.
Where does it start and end?
It starts in Oviedo in Asturias, cuts through the mountains and meets the Francés at Melide, from where the last days are shared in towards Santiago.
Is the Camino Primitivo hard?
Yes, it is considered the hardest Camino. It is short in kilometres, but almost a constant run of mountain passes, with several days of 25 to 27 kilometres in demanding terrain.
When is the best time of year?
Late May to early October. The mountain stages are exposed, so keep an eye on the forecast, especially around the Hospitales variant.
Roughly how much does it cost?
If you travel on a budget and sleep in hostels, a day can cost around 20 to 30 euros.
What is the Hospitales route?
It is the high variant across the ridge past the ruins of medieval pilgrim hospitals. Spectacular in clear weather, but exposed in mist and rain, where the low route is the safe alternative.
