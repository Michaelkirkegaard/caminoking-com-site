Getting to the Camino: how to reach your start town

How to reach the Camino's start towns by plane, train and bus: Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, Pamplona, Sarria, Porto, Ferrol, Oviedo and Irún. And home again.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 7 min read

You fly to an airport near your start town and cover the last leg by train or bus. Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port is reached either via Bayonne by regional train or via Pamplona by bus. Monbus runs daily from Santiago to Sarria with a stop at Santiago airport, and Renfe has trains from Madrid.

Getting home from Santiago is simpler. City bus 6A runs to the airport (SCQ), and the train and bus stations sit together about ten minutes on foot from Praza de Galicia. Flights from your home airport change with the season, so check the airport’s own list of destinations before you book.

Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port

Via Bayonne and Biarritz

TER Nouvelle-Aquitaine, SNCF’s regional rail service, runs the trains between Bayonne and Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port. The journey takes around an hour.

If you fly to Biarritz Pays Basque, the Txik Txak city bus line 3 runs from the airport to Bayonne station. The airport states that the line runs every day from early morning until late evening, every 15 minutes on weekdays, every 20 minutes on Saturdays and every half hour on Sundays.

Express Bourricot in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port runs shuttles for one to eight people, including to and from Biarritz airport, Pamplona and Roncesvalles. Every seat has to be booked in advance. The office in town is closed from 17 October 2026 to 7 March 2027, and during that period you book by email or through the online form.

Via Pamplona

ALSA has both Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and Roncesvalles on its list of Camino destinations. Departures between Pamplona and Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port are not fixed all year round, so search for your travel date with ALSA before you book a flight.

If you want to start in Roncesvalles, Autocares Artieda runs a scheduled bus from Pamplona bus station at Calle Yanguas y Miranda 2. The journey takes about one hour and ten minutes. The bus runs Monday to Saturday, not on Sundays and public holidays, and the ticket is bought on the bus before departure, in cash or by card.

Pamplona

According to Aena, Pamplona airport is six kilometres from the city and has a permanent taxi rank. Renfe runs Alvia trains between Madrid and Pamplona. Biarritz airport also mentions cross-border coaches to Pamplona, San Sebastián and Bilbao with ALSA/Conda, BlaBlaCar and Flixbus.

Sarria

Sarria was the starting point for 161,901 pilgrims in 2025 according to the pilgrim office in Santiago, more than any other town. The connections are good as a result:

  • From Santiago: Monbus runs daily between Santiago and Sarria, with a stop at the airport.
  • Via Lugo: Monbus has more than ten daily departures between Santiago and Lugo and a route between Lugo and Sarria. From the airport, Monbus runs to Lugo with stops in Arzúa, Melide and Palas de Rei, among others.
  • From Madrid: Renfe sells trains from Madrid Chamartín Clara Campoamor to Sarria.

The walk itself is described in the guide to the last 100 km from Sarria.

Porto

Porto is the most used starting point on the Camino Portugués when the central route and the coastal route are added together: 26,489 and 25,091 pilgrims in 2025. From the airport, metro line E, the violet line, runs through the city to Estádio do Dragão. The airport states that there is a departure every 20 or 30 minutes depending on the time and the day of the week.

Ferrol and A Coruña

For the great majority, the Camino Inglés begins in Ferrol, which 28,886 pilgrims gave as their starting point in 2025. Monbus runs from Santiago to both Ferrol and A Coruña, and Renfe has regional trains between A Coruña and Ferrol. From A Coruña airport, ALSA’s line A4 runs into the city.

Oviedo

Oviedo is the most used starting point on the Camino Primitivo, with 15,359 pilgrims in 2025. From Asturias airport, ALSA runs to Oviedo, Gijón and Avilés. Aena states that buses leave the airport every day between 6:00 and 00:00, about once an hour.

Irún

Irún is a start town on the Camino del Norte, and 5,365 pilgrims gave it as their starting point in 2025. San Sebastián airport is in Hondarribia, 22 kilometres north-east of San Sebastián. Lurraldebus runs from the airport to Irún, Hondarribia and San Sebastián. Renfe has Alvia trains on the line Madrid, San Sebastián, Irún.

Getting home from Santiago

The airport

Santiago’s airport is called Santiago-Rosalía de Castro and has the code SCQ. City bus 6A links the city and the airport and is operated by Tralusa, according to Aena. Gronze states that the bus runs every 20 or 30 minutes, stops at the bus station, Praza de Galicia and the train station, and that the trip takes 30 to 45 minutes depending on the stop. The airport has a permanent taxi rank. Monbus also runs from the airport to A Coruña and Lugo.

Train and bus

The train station is next to the bus station, and together the two are known as the Estación Intermodal. It is about ten minutes on foot from Praza de Galicia along Rúa do Hórreo. Renfe runs AVE and other long-distance trains between Santiago and Madrid Chamartín Clara Campoamor, and Gronze gives the journey time as about three hours.

ALSA runs long-distance coaches from Santiago and gives a 15 per cent discount on the journey home on selected routes when you show your pilgrim passport. The discount only applies when you buy at one of the company’s points of sale, including the one in the pilgrim office at Rúa das Carretas 33. More on the last day in the guide to arriving in Santiago.

Three things to check before you book

  • The season. Timetables change with the time of year, and not every connection runs all year round. Always search for your own date.
  • The day of the week. The bus to Roncesvalles does not run on Sundays and public holidays.
  • The connection. Flights, trains and buses are sold separately. Leave plenty of time between them, or plan a night in Bayonne, Pamplona or Santiago.

Frequently asked questions

How do you get to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port?
There are two ways. The French one goes via Bayonne, where TER Nouvelle-Aquitaine runs regional trains to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port. From Biarritz airport, the Txik Txak city bus line 3 runs to Bayonne station. The Spanish one goes via Pamplona, where ALSA lists Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port among its destinations. Express Bourricot also runs shuttles, which have to be booked in advance.
How do you get from Santiago to Sarria?
The bus company Monbus runs daily from Santiago to Sarria, and the route stops at Santiago airport. You can also take Monbus from Santiago or the airport to Lugo and change to the company's bus between Lugo and Sarria. If you travel via Madrid, Renfe sells train tickets from Madrid Chamartín Clara Campoamor to Sarria.
How do you get from Santiago to the airport?
City bus line 6A runs between the city and Santiago-Rosalía de Castro airport. According to the airport operator Aena, the line is operated by Tralusa. The guide site Gronze states that the bus runs every 20 or 30 minutes, stops at the bus station, Praza de Galicia and the train station, and takes 30 to 45 minutes depending on the stop. The airport also has a permanent taxi rank.
Which airport should you fly to for the Camino?
It depends on the start town. Biarritz and Pamplona are closest to Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port. Santiago is used for Sarria, Porto for the Portuguese route, A Coruña for Ferrol, Asturias for Oviedo and San Sebastián for Irún. Which flights exist from your home airport changes with the season, so check the airport's own list of destinations before you book.
Can you buy a bus ticket to Roncesvalles online?
Not from Autocares Artieda, which runs the scheduled bus between Pamplona and Roncesvalles. The company states that tickets are sold directly on the bus before each departure, and that you can pay in cash or by card. The bus leaves from Pamplona bus station Monday to Saturday and takes about one hour and ten minutes. It does not run on Sundays and public holidays.
