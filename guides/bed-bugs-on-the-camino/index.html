Bed bugs on the Camino: how to avoid and handle them

Bed bugs do not spread disease, but they itch. See how to check a bed, what a 60 degree wash and a hot dryer do, and how to keep them out of your home.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

  5. How not to bring them home

Bed bugs are unpleasant, but they are not dangerous. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) states that bed bugs are not known to spread diseases to people, and the bites usually clear up on their own in a week or so.

The best way to avoid them is to check the bed before you unpack and to keep your backpack closed. If you get them anyway, the answer is heat: a wash at 60 degrees Celsius and a hot tumble dryer for at least 30 minutes.

What bed bugs are

Bed bugs are small, flat, reddish-brown insects without wings. According to the CDC they are between 1 and 7 millimetres long, and the UK National Health Service (NHS) gives around 5 millimetres for adults. They bite people at night and hide during the day in mattress seams, bed frames, cracks and crevices. The CDC writes that they tend to live within 8 feet, about 2.5 metres, of where people sleep.

The pest laboratory at Aarhus University in Denmark writes that bed bugs are spread predominantly with clothing, luggage and furniture. That is why they can turn up anywhere many travellers sleep one after another, and the CDC names hotels as one example. This article gives no figures for how common they are on the Camino, because no credible count was found.

How to recognise bites and signs

SignWhat it looks like
BitesRaised, itchy marks, often in a line or a group, on skin that was uncovered during the night
Blood spotsSmall rust-coloured spots on the sheet or mattress
DroppingsSmall brown or black spots on bedding, mattress or furniture
SkinsEmpty skins shed by bugs that have moulted
BugsLive bed bugs in the folds and seams of mattress and sheet
SmellA sweet, musty odour

The bites look similar to mosquito and flea bites. According to the CDC, most people do not notice the marks until one to several days after the bite, and for some it can take up to 14 days. The Aarhus laboratory writes that people differ greatly in how sensitive they are, and that some are not bothered at all. Bites alone are therefore not enough to establish that bed bugs are present. Look for the signs in the bed.

Prevention backed by evidence

The CDC’s advice for travellers is short and can be used directly in a pilgrim hostel:

  • Look carefully at the mattress, bedding and furniture before you unpack. Eggs and young bugs are very small and easy to overlook.
  • Find somewhere else to sleep if you see bugs or signs such as blood spots on the sheet.
  • Keep your luggage off the floor, and closed when you are not using it.
  • Take out only what you need, and inspect clothing before it goes back in.

Since the bugs hide in mattress seams and bed frames, it also makes sense to keep the backpack away from the bed itself. A sleeping bag liner or a sleeping bag that can be washed at 60 degrees is easy to treat along the way. Check the care label before you buy.

Permethrin

Permethrin is an insecticide in the pyrethroid group. It is approved in the EU as an active substance in insecticides by Commission Implementing Regulation 1090/2014. Which products may be sold depends on the country, and they must be used according to the label.

No health authority was found that recommends treating a sleeping bag or backpack against bed bugs. The CDC’s travel advice on bed bugs does not mention permethrin, and the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) states that some bed bug populations have become resistant to pyrethroids.

If you get bed bugs

  1. Tell the hospitalero straight away, so the bed can be inspected and treated and the next pilgrims are spared.
  2. Put clothes and sleeping gear in a sealed plastic bag until they can be washed. The EPA recommends transporting laundry in plastic bags.
  3. Wash at 60 degrees and tumble dry on a hot setting for at least 30 minutes.
  4. Empty the backpack and check seams, pockets and straps. Whatever cannot be washed stays in a sealed bag until it can be heated or frozen.
MethodTemperature and timeSource
Washing60 degreesNHS, Naylor and Boase 2010
Tumble dryerHot setting for at least 30 minutesNHS, Naylor and Boase 2010
Other heat48 degrees for 71.5 minutes, or at least 50 degrees everywhere the bugs can hideKells and Goblirsch 2011
FreezingMinus 18 degrees for three daysEPA

The eggs are the most tolerant. In the 2011 study, 99 per cent of adults died at 48.3 degrees, while the eggs required 54.8 degrees. The EPA mentions that a black plastic bag in a hot, closed car in the sun can work, but that the result depends on the climate. That method is therefore uncertain for a backpack, and a hot tumble dryer is the safe choice for everything that can take it.

For the itching, the NHS advises something cool, such as a clean, damp cloth. A pharmacist can suggest a mild hydrocortisone cream or antihistamines. See a doctor if the bites are still very painful, swollen or itchy after treatment, or if the swelling spreads. How to see a doctor in Spain is covered in the guide to insurance, health and safety on the Camino.

How not to bring them home

The Aarhus laboratory advises going through your luggage when you get home after a night somewhere bed bugs were present or suspected. Do it outside the bedroom, and go through everything on your packing list.

  • Wash everything that can take it at 60 degrees and dry it on a hot setting for at least 30 minutes.
  • Freeze what cannot be washed at minus 18 degrees. The Aarhus laboratory gives a couple of days and the EPA three days. The EPA recommends measuring the temperature with a thermometer, because not every freezer is cold enough.
  • Check the backpack thoroughly in seams and pockets.
  • Items that can be neither washed, heated nor frozen can, according to the EPA, be left in a sealed plastic bag or container for up to a year.

Putting the backpack in a cupboard and waiting is not a solution. According to the Aarhus laboratory, an adult bed bug can manage a couple of months without blood at room temperature.

This article is general information and not medical advice.

Frequently asked questions

Are bed bugs dangerous?
No, not in the sense of infection. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) states that bed bugs are not known to spread diseases to people, and the pest laboratory at Aarhus University writes that they do not seem to play any role in spreading infection. The bites can itch a lot and disturb sleep, and a few people react strongly, but they usually clear up on their own in a week or so.
What do bed bug bites look like?
The bites are raised and itchy and often sit in a line or a group on skin that was uncovered during the night, such as the face, neck and arms. They look similar to mosquito or flea bites. According to the CDC, most people do not notice the marks until one to several days after the bite, and for some it can take up to 14 days. Some people do not react at all.
What temperature kills bed bugs?
The UK National Health Service (NHS) recommends a hot wash at 60 degrees Celsius and tumble drying on a hot setting for at least 30 minutes. A 2010 study found that washing at 60 degrees killed all life stages. A 2011 study found that 99 per cent of adults die at 48.3 degrees and 99 per cent of eggs at 54.8 degrees, so the eggs are the most heat tolerant.
What do you do if you find bed bugs in a pilgrim hostel?
Tell the hospitalero straight away so the bed can be inspected and treated, and ask for another bed or another room. Put clothes and sleeping gear in a sealed plastic bag until you can wash them at 60 degrees and dry them on a hot setting for at least 30 minutes. Empty the backpack and check seams and pockets before you pack again.
Does permethrin work against bed bugs?
No health authority was found that recommends treating a sleeping bag or backpack with permethrin against bed bugs. The substance is approved in the EU as an active substance in insecticides, but the CDC's travel advice on bed bugs does not mention it, and the US Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) states that some bed bug populations have become resistant to the group of substances permethrin belongs to. Heat is the best documented method.
How do you avoid bringing bed bugs home?
Go through your luggage when you get home, and do it outside the bedroom. Wash everything that can take it at 60 degrees and dry it on a hot setting for at least 30 minutes. Items that cannot be washed can be frozen at minus 18 degrees for a couple of days according to the pest laboratory at Aarhus University. The EPA gives three days at the same temperature and recommends checking the freezer with a thermometer.
