Walking Hærvejen: Denmark's ancient pilgrim road

Guide to Hærvejen, the ancient road through Jutland in Denmark. Around 275 to 290 km from Viborg to the German border, with stages, history and my advice.

Hærvejen is one of Denmark’s great pilgrim routes, and for me it is one of the most beautiful places to walk in the whole country. The route follows the ancient road that for centuries ran through Jutland, the mainland peninsula of Denmark, along the watershed where travellers, pilgrims and traders could move with dry feet. Today it has become a continuous walking route of around 275 to 290 kilometres, and it still carries its history in every footstep.

When I walk on Hærvejen, I feel some of the same calm as on the Camino in Spain. There is the same rhythm, the same long horizon and the same feeling of being a small part of a very old story. Here I share what the route is, how it is divided up, and how to get the most out of the trip.

What is Hærvejen?

Hærvejen, sometimes translated as the Army Road or the Ancient Road, is the name of the old road that ran down the ridge of Jutland. The name covers several tracks that have woven in and out of each other over time, but the basic idea is the same. Travellers followed the high, dry ground where it was easiest to move forward, avoiding the wide river valleys and the wet meadows.

The road has been used by cattle drovers, traders, armies and pilgrims. The pilgrims are the reason the route is often called a Danish Camino today. Many walked along Hærvejen on their way out into Europe, onwards to the great pilgrim destinations, and some all the way to Santiago de Compostela.

Today Hærvejen is signposted as a modern walking and cycling route. You follow a yellow pilgrim symbol and good markings, and you can walk the whole route in one go or split it into smaller trips over several seasons.

How long is Hærvejen, and where does it go?

The walking route runs around 275 to 290 kilometres. Most people walk from Viborg heading south and finish at the border around Padborg. Along the way there are both eastern and western alternative sections on some stretches, and that is one of the reasons the total length varies a little from map to map.

You choose the direction yourself. Some prefer to walk from north to south with the border as their goal, while others turn it around. There is no right or wrong, and the landscape is beautiful both ways.

Here is a rough overview of the route from north to south:

Part of the routeLandscapeApproximate length
The Viborg area heading southHeath, forest and old road tracksaround 70 to 90 km
Central JutlandRiver valleys, springs and small hillsaround 90 to 110 km
Towards the border at PadborgForest, lakes and open countryaround 90 to 110 km

The numbers are approximate and should be read as a guide rather than a fixed answer. If you choose the eastern branch in one place and the western in another, the total distance shifts a little.

Stages and pace

I like to set my own pace, and that goes for Hærvejen too. If you walk around 20 to 25 kilometres a day, the whole route takes around 12 to 14 days. If you would rather have short days and plenty of time to look at churches, burial mounds and nature, you can stretch the trip over several weeks or split it into pieces.

Some of the things I love most along the way:

  • The old Hærvejen inns, where you can get a meal and rest your legs
  • The heathland that opens up and brings an enormous calm
  • The many springs, river valleys and rolling hills of central Jutland
  • The historical traces, from burial mounds to old stone bridges across the streams

Because the route is so long, most people plan around accommodation. It is a good idea to study the map and set your daily stages so you end up somewhere you can sleep well.

Accommodation along Hærvejen

There are plenty of places to stay along the way, and you can choose the level that suits your trip. Along the route you will find:

  • Shelters and simple camping spots for those who want to sleep close to nature
  • Hostels
  • Inns, hotels and bed and breakfasts
  • Campsites

If you want to travel light and cheap, you can combine shelters with the occasional night under a roof. If you would rather have a bed and a hot shower every evening, that works too, but then it is wise to book well in advance in high season.

Is Hærvejen hard to walk?

The terrain is soft and typically Jutlandic. There are no mountains, but there are long days, and the weather can change quickly. After rain some paths can be wet and muddy, so a good pair of hiking boots and sensible rain gear make a big difference.

The route is well signposted, and with a map or a good app in your back pocket few people get lost. The biggest challenge is usually endurance over many days in a row rather than the terrain itself. That makes it a fine route to train up for if you want to walk it in one go.

Hærvejen as a pilgrim route

For me, Hærvejen is more than a walking route. It is a pilgrim route with deep history, and it is becoming more and more popular among people who want to try a Camino without travelling far. You can get a Danish pilgrim passport and collect stamps along the way, just like on the Camino in Spain, and many use the trip to switch off completely from everyday life.

If you want to read more about the Danish pilgrim network and the other routes, I have gathered them all in my overview of Danish Camino routes. If the coast calls you more, take a look at Gendarmstien in southern Jutland, another of my Danish favourites. And if you are curious about the cycling event that shares the landscape, I have written about the Hærvejen Race separately.

Hærvejen is a trip I always recommend. It is long enough to give you the true pilgrim feeling, and at the same time it is right outside the door. Lace up your boots, follow the yellow symbol, and let the old road carry you through Jutland.

Frequently asked questions

How long is Hærvejen?
The classic pilgrim route on Hærvejen runs around 275 to 290 kilometres from Viborg towards Padborg and the Danish-German border. The length varies a little depending on which detours and alternative sections you choose.
Where does Hærvejen start and end?
Most people walk from Viborg heading south and finish at the border around Padborg. You can easily reverse the direction and walk from south to north, and many split the route into several trips over time.
How long does it take to walk Hærvejen?
At a calm pace of around 20 to 25 kilometres a day, the whole of Hærvejen takes around 12 to 14 days. If you walk longer stages you can finish faster, but most people enjoy having plenty of time.
Is Hærvejen hard to walk?
The terrain is soft Jutland landscape with heath, forest, river valleys and small hills. There are no mountains, but long days and changing weather can be a challenge. The route is well signposted with the yellow pilgrim symbol.
Can you stay overnight along Hærvejen?
Yes. There are shelters, camping spots, hostels, inns and bed and breakfasts along the whole route. Many plan their daily stages around where they want to sleep and book the more popular places well in advance.
What is the difference between Hærvejen and the Hærvejen Race?
Hærvejen is the old walking and pilgrim route through Jutland. The Hærvejen Race is a cycling and running event that uses parts of the same landscape. This guide is entirely about the walking route.
