When to walk the Camino, month by month

Weather, crowds and closed albergues month by month: May, June and September suit most first-timers, while winter takes more planning. See the figures.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Most people get the best first Camino in May, June or September. Days are warm without being hot on most of the Camino Francés, the albergues are open and every route can be walked. For more space around you, April and October are quieter, but also cooler and wetter.

July and August are the warmest and driest months, but busy. The busiest month in 2025 was September, with about 81,500 pilgrims registered in Santiago. From November to March there are few pilgrims, many albergues close, and the Napoleon route over the Pyrenees is shut from 1 November to 31 March.

Burgos and León sit high, at 891 and 916 metres, so nights are cool even in summer. Santiago is wet: the weather station records an average of 1,787 mm of precipitation a year, and even in July it rains on five to six days.

The pilgrim figures count arrivals in Santiago. If you walk the whole Camino Francés in around five weeks, you meet the crowds on the early stages some weeks before the month shown in the table. Around 83 per cent of pilgrims in 2025 arrived between May and October.

Spring: March to May

March is still winter on the high plateau. Burgos averages 12 days of frost in March and just under 7 in April, and in Santiago it rains on 14 days in April. In return there is plenty of space: in 2025 about 14,100 pilgrims arrived in March against about 54,600 in April.

For many people May is the best month. Daytime temperatures are around 18 to 21 degrees, but Burgos gets as much rain in May as in December. Pack rain gear, and see the packing list.

Summer: June to August

June has long, warm days and less rain. In July and August daytime highs reach 27 to 28 degrees in Pamplona, Burgos and León. Those are averages, so some days are hotter. Start early and walk the open stretches across the plateau in the morning. Santiago is milder at about 24 to 25 degrees.

August was the third busiest month in 2025. The last 100 km from Sarria are especially busy, because around 31 per cent of all pilgrims start there.

Autumn: September and October

September has summer warmth without the hottest days, and many people know it. It was the busiest month in 2025. October is quieter, but the rain returns in Galicia: Santiago averages 226 mm in October against 107 mm in September. On the plateau, night temperatures drop to around 6 to 7 degrees.

Winter: November to February

From November to March just under 7 per cent of the pilgrims of 2025 arrived. Burgos and León have frost on around 18 days in January and snow on four to five, and Santiago gets more than 200 mm of rain a month from October to January.

Many albergues close for the winter months. The public albergues stay open all year, especially once you enter Galicia, but opening times vary from place to place. Phone or message each place you plan to stay, and bring a warm sleeping bag.

The Napoleon route, the eastern variant from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port over the Lepoeder pass to Roncesvalles, is closed from 1 November to 31 March. The decision is made each year by the Dirección General de Interior of the regional government of Navarre, and the measure has existed since 2015, after many rescue operations in the area. During the closure it is mandatory to walk the western variant through Luzaide/Valcarlos and the Ibañeta pass. Read more about where to start the Camino Francés.

Holy Years: 2027 will be busy

A Holy Year in Santiago falls whenever the feast of Saint James on 25 July is a Sunday. That happens at intervals of 6, 5, 6 and 11 years. The most recent Holy Years were 1993, 1999, 2004, 2010 and 2021 to 2022, and the next is 2027.

Pilgrim numbers set a record in 2025 at about 531,000, which was 6 per cent more than in 2024. If you walk in 2027, expect busy routes and plan accommodation earlier than you otherwise would.

The month has to suit the route

The figures above apply to the Camino Francés. On the Vía de la Plata, which starts in Seville, summer is a poor choice: Seville has an average daily high of 36 degrees in July. The Spanish postal service Correos recommends March, April, May and October for that route and advises against July, August and September. If the route is still undecided, start with the guide to which Camino route to choose.

Frequently asked questions

Which month is best for a first Camino?
May, June and September suit most people best. Days are warm on most of the Camino Francés, the albergues are open and every route can be walked. These are also busy months. In 2025 the pilgrim office in Santiago registered about 78,600 pilgrims in May and about 81,500 in September. For more space, April and October are quieter, but cooler and wetter.
When is the Camino most crowded?
In 2025 September was the busiest month, with about 81,500 pilgrims registered in Santiago, followed by May with about 78,600 and August with about 77,100. Around 83 per cent of the year's pilgrims arrived between May and October. January, February and December each stayed below 4,000. The figures count arrivals in Santiago, so crowding further back on the route comes some weeks earlier.
Can you walk the Camino in winter?
Yes, but it takes more planning. Many albergues close for the winter months, while the public albergues, especially in Galicia, stay open all year. The Napoleon route over the Pyrenees is closed from 1 November to 31 March, so you have to walk via Valcarlos. Frost is common on the high plateau around Burgos and León, and Galicia gets a lot of rain. Check opening times for each place before you set out.
When is the Napoleon route closed?
The eastern variant between Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and Roncesvalles over the Lepoeder pass is closed every winter from 1 November to 31 March. The decision is made by the regional government of Navarre, through its Dirección General de Interior, and has applied since 2015. During the closure the western variant through Luzaide/Valcarlos and the Ibañeta pass is mandatory. The closure applies whatever the weather on the day.
When is the next Holy Year in Santiago?
The next Holy Year is 2027. A Holy Year in Santiago falls whenever the feast of Saint James on 25 July is a Sunday, which happens at intervals of 6, 5, 6 and 11 years. The most recent were 1993, 1999, 2004, 2010 and 2021 to 2022. Pilgrim numbers set a record in 2025, so expect busy routes in 2027 and plan accommodation well ahead.
