Arriving in Santiago de Compostela: what happens at the end

In Santiago the pilgrim office, your Compostela and the Pilgrim's Mass await. See addresses, opening hours, fees and the backpack rule in the cathedral.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

When you walk into Praza do Obradoiro in front of the cathedral, the walk is over. After that there are three things most people do: collect the Compostela at the pilgrim office at Rúa Carretas 33, attend the Pilgrim’s Mass and visit the cathedral. The Compostela is free. The certificate of distance costs 3 euros.

Two practical points are worth knowing in advance. You cannot take your backpack into the cathedral, and in busy periods the pilgrim office cannot promise that you will get your Compostela on the same day. So allow a full day in the city if you can. Everything below was checked on the cathedral’s and the pilgrim office’s own pages on 20 November 2026.

Praza do Obradoiro: where the routes end

The square lies in front of the cathedral’s Baroque west facade. According to the city’s tourist office, a small granite plaque in the middle of the square marks the place where the routes end. Pilgrims call it kilometre zero.

Four buildings stand around the square: the cathedral, the Hostal dos Reis Católicos, built from 1501 as a royal hospital and a parador since 1954, the Colexio de San Xerome, which houses the university’s rectorate, and the Pazo de Raxoi from 1787, which is now the city hall.

The Pilgrim’s Reception Office

The Oficina de Acogida al Peregrino is run by the cathedral. Here you get the cathedral’s final stamp in your pilgrim passport and your Compostela, if you meet the conditions.

WhatInformation on 20 November 2026
AddressRúa Carretas 33, 15705 Santiago de Compostela

Opening hoursEvery day from 10.00. The Spanish pages say until 18.00, the English pages until 19.00

The office’s own questions and answers page mentions longer summer hours, 8.00 to 20.00 from Easter Monday to 31 October. The three statements do not agree, so check the front page just before you arrive and plan around the shortest hours. If you arrive on one of the two closed days, the office says you can get your Compostela in the cathedral.

How the queue works

To collect the Compostela you must take a ticket with a QR code. The code lets you follow the queue in real time on your phone. The office warns that in periods of great influx it cannot guarantee collection on the same day.

The office’s website has a registration form for individual pilgrims and one for groups. Follow the instructions on the form.

The Compostela and the certificate of distance

The Compostela is the cathedral’s certificate that you have completed the pilgrimage. In short, the conditions are:

  • at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot or on horseback on the same route, or 200 km by bicycle
  • the final stage into the cathedral must be included
  • at least two stamps a day for at least the last 100 km if you are walking

The rules are covered in the article on the last 100 km from Sarria. The background to the pilgrimage is in the main guide to the Camino de Santiago.

Certificate of distance3 eurosRecords starting date, starting place, kilometres covered, arrival date and route

The certificate of distance is issued by the cathedral chapter. If you have already completed the walk, you can also request it afterwards by email from the cathedral.

The Pilgrim’s Mass

The cathedral publishes these times for the Pilgrim’s Mass:

TimePlace on 20 November 2026
7.30Chapel of the Communion in the cathedral
9.30Church of San Agustín
12.00Church of San Agustín
19.30Church of San Agustín

The last three Masses are temporarily held in San Agustín because of works on the main altar. The page does not say how long this will last, so check the cathedral’s site when you arrive.

The botafumeiro

The botafumeiro is the cathedral’s great censer. According to the cathedral it is used at the main solemnities, during the entrance procession or at the end of Mass: 6 January, Easter Sunday, Ascension, 23 May, Pentecost, 25 July, 15 August, 1 November, Christ the King, 8 December, 25 December and 30 December.

Pilgrim groups can request the botafumeiro by writing to botafumeiro@catedraldesantiago.es. The cathedral’s page does not state a fee for it.

On 20 November 2026 the cathedral’s page says that the botafumeiro is temporarily not operating because of restoration work.

The cathedral and your backpack

The cathedral is open every day from 7.00 to 21.00. Entry is free, and the entrance is the Puerta de Platerías. In summer it closes at 20.30 on Saturdays, when a vigil is held with the pilgrims. There are no visits during services. Inside, pilgrims climb the stairs in the ambulatory and embrace the figure of the Apostle.

Backpacks are not allowed for security reasons. You can leave yours where you are staying, or use the Correos luggage storage at the main post office at Rúa do Franco 4, a two-minute walk from the cathedral according to Correos.

From 31 October 2026 to 15 March 2027 it is open Monday to Friday from 8.30 to 20.30 and on Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays from 10.00 to 18.00. From 16 March to 31 October 2027 it is open every day from 8.30 to 20.30. It is closed on 25 December and on 1 and 6 January. If you have had luggage sent ahead, read about Camino luggage transfer.

On to Fisterra and Muxía

Many continue to the Atlantic along the Camino Finisterre. Gronze gives these distances:

  • Santiago to Fisterra: 86.7 km
  • Santiago to Muxía: 85.6 km
  • between Fisterra and Muxía: 27.8 km
  • both towns on the same trip: 113 to 117 km, four to five walking days

In Fisterra you can get the Fisterrana certificate free of charge by showing your stamped pilgrim passport at the tourist office on Plaza de la Constitución or at the public hostel. In Muxía the certificate is called the Muxiana, and you can ask for it at the tourist office or the municipal hostel.

Frequently asked questions

Where do you collect your Compostela in Santiago?
You collect it at the Pilgrim's Reception Office, the Oficina de Acogida al Peregrino, at Rúa Carretas 33. The office is close to Praza do Obradoiro, and you reach it along Rúa Costa do Cristo. You take a ticket with a QR code, follow the queue on your phone and show your stamped pilgrim passport when your turn comes. The cathedral's final stamp is given here too.
Does the Compostela cost anything?
No. The pilgrim office states that both the reception of pilgrims and the issuing of the Compostela are completely free. The certificate of distance is optional and costs 3 euros. It records your starting date and place, the kilometres covered, your arrival date and the route. Income from the certificate helps to fund the office. The fees were checked on 20 November 2026.
Can you take your backpack into the cathedral?
No. The cathedral states that access with backpacks is not allowed for security reasons. You can leave it where you are staying, or use the Correos luggage storage at Rúa do Franco 4, which Correos says is a two-minute walk from the cathedral. In 2026 a backpack or small suitcase costs 4 euros a day and a walking pole 1 euro.
When is the Pilgrim's Mass in Santiago?
The cathedral lists four daily Pilgrim's Masses: at 7.30, 9.30, 12.00 and 19.30. On 20 November 2026 the 7.30 Mass is held in the Chapel of the Communion in the cathedral, while the other three are temporarily moved to the Church of San Agustín because of works on the main altar. Check the cathedral's liturgy page just before you arrive, as this can change.
When can you see the botafumeiro?
According to the cathedral, the great censer is used at the main solemnities of the church year, for example 25 July, 15 August, 1 November and 25 December. Pilgrim groups can request it by email. On 20 November 2026, however, the cathedral states that the botafumeiro is temporarily out of operation because of restoration work, so do not count on seeing it until the cathedral says otherwise.
Can you walk on from Santiago to the sea?
Yes. According to Gronze it is 86.7 km from Santiago to Fisterra and 85.6 km to Muxía, and the stretch between the two towns is 27.8 km. To see both, allow 113 to 117 km and four to five walking days. Each town has its own certificate, the Fisterrana and the Muxiana, which you get by showing your stamped pilgrim passport.
