How long is the Camino de Santiago? Every route in km

How long is the Camino? The Camino Francés is around 770 km and takes 25 to 35 days. See distance and stages for all seven routes, and the last 100 km.

One of the questions I get most often is how long it takes to walk the Camino. The first thing I always point out is that there are many different Caminos, but in 95 percent of cases people are asking about the Camino Frances. So here is the quick answer.

How long does it take to walk the Camino? It depends on how fast you walk and which route you choose, but the Camino Frances is around 770 kilometres and usually takes between 25 and 35 days. Divided into 31 stages, it takes 31 days if you follow that itinerary.

Want to know more about the distance, the terrain and the individual stages? Keep reading. Below you will find the length of all seven routes and the full list of stages on the French route.

The last 100 km

You do not have to walk a whole route. The pilgrim certificate, the Compostela, requires at least 100 km on foot, which is why so many start in Sarria on the Camino Francés. See the last 100 km from Sarria and where to start the Camino Francés.

How long is the Camino?

The Camino de Santiago is a huge pilgrim network with routes all over Europe, in many different lengths. There is no single answer to how long “the Camino” is, but the French route is roughly 750 to about 770 kilometres, and since it is by far the best-known Camino in the world, that is the route this article covers. If you want an overview of the other well-known routes in Spain, have a look at the guides section.

Most Caminos have alternative paths you can take, so there is never one exact distance. People usually say the Camino Frances is about 770 kilometres and takes about 30 days.

A few useful numbers if you follow the list below:

  • The route is divided into 31 stages, so the standard itinerary takes 31 days
  • The longest stage is around 37 kilometres
  • The shortest stage is around 19 kilometres
  • You need to average about 25 kilometres per day to finish in 31 days

The stages of the Camino Frances

Many people get a shock on day one. The first stage is clearly the hardest, crossing the Pyrenees from France into Spain. It is also many pilgrims’ favourite, because you climb high into bare mountains. More mountain stages come later in the route, but by then you are used to the terrain and to walking long distances. Challenges can still show up on plenty of stages.

Here is the French route divided into 31 stages, with distance and the number of albergues (pilgrim hostels) on each stage. Stages are suggestions. The page on the Camino Francés shows the other common division into 33 stages, the one the Gronze guide uses.

A little history of the French route

To see the routes on a map, go to Camino de Santiago map with all seven routes, free maps and GPS tracks.

The French route has been the most popular Camino ever since it was discovered that St. James, one of the twelve apostles, lay buried in the cathedral in Santiago de Compostela. It has been one of the most important Christian pilgrimage routes for the last 1,000 years.

Today the route is walked by hikers from all over the world. Most people you meet walk it for spiritual reasons, though not necessarily religious ones. The route starts in France and runs almost entirely through northern Spain.

What is the terrain like?

You walk roughly 770 kilometres across Spain, and a question I often get is what kind of ground you walk on. Some people are surprised that parts of the route follow paved roads, and others are surprised that you cross real mountains. When you walk roughly 770 kilometres through Spain, you meet a bit of everything.

Some areas are stunningly beautiful. You also pass through plenty of towns that look unremarkable at first glance, yet the French route is full of good atmosphere and culture, and I kept finding hidden gems. You may well find yourself alone on a 25 kilometre gravel track, and that can be a fantastic experience in itself.

To the end of the world

If 31 days feels like too little, you can extend the walk beyond Santiago to Finisterre on the Atlantic coast, a place once considered the end of the world. And if 775 kilometres feels like too much, remember that you do not have to walk the whole route. Many pilgrims start further along and walk the final stretch to Santiago.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the Camino de Santiago in kilometres?
It depends on the route. Rounded from several sources, the Camino Francés is about 770 km, the Camino del Norte about 830 km, the Vía de la Plata about 980 km, the Camino Primitivo about 320 km, the Camino Portugués about 610 km from Lisbon and about 240 km from Porto on the central route, the Camino Inglés about 115 km and the Camino Finisterre about 90 km. The sources are listed on each route page.
How many stages is the Camino?
The Camino Francés is divided into 33 stages by Gronze and 31 by Correos. The other routes run from 4 stages (Camino Finisterre) to 37 (Vía de la Plata) in Gronze. One stage is one walking day, so add rest days on top.
How long does it take to walk the whole Camino?
Allow 25 to 35 days for the whole Camino Francés, depending on your pace and how many rest days you take.
What is the shortest way to earn a Compostela?
Walk the last 100 kilometres, most often from Sarria to Santiago. The pilgrim office requires at least 100 km on foot.
