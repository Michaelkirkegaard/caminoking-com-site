Insurance, health and safety on the Camino

The European Health Insurance Card covers public healthcare in Spain but not the journey home. Here is insurance, 112, AlertCops, heat and road walking.

Carry your European Health Insurance Card if you are entitled to one, and buy travel insurance as well. The card gives you public healthcare in Spain on the same terms as people insured there, but it covers neither the journey home nor private doctors and hospitals. Readers from outside the EU and the other participating countries have no card to fall back on and need their own cover.

Safety comes down to a few simple habits: call 112 in an emergency, keep your phone charged, walk in daylight, and make yourself visible when the route follows a road. The Spanish authorities also run services designed for pilgrims, including the AlertCops app.

The European Health Insurance Card

The card is free and is issued by your national health insurance provider. According to the European Commission it gives access to medically necessary, state-provided healthcare during a temporary stay in the 27 EU countries, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland or the United Kingdom, under the same conditions and at the same cost as for people insured in that country. If Spanish patients pay part of a service themselves, so do you.

The card coversThe card does not cover
Necessary treatment from doctors and hospitals in the public systemTravel home, such as a return flight
Treatment on the same terms as people insured in the countryPrivate doctors, clinics and hospitals
Prescription medicine with a patient chargeTreatment that is the express purpose of the trip

How you order the card depends on the country where you are insured, so check with your national provider well before departure.

Why you still need travel insurance

The European Commission states that the card is not an alternative to travel insurance. Lost and stolen property is not covered either.

Look for four things when you compare policies: transport home, treatment at private clinics, dental treatment and cover for luggage. Dental care is generally not part of the public service for adults in Spain. Prices and terms vary, so read the conditions and check in particular whether the policy covers a walking trip of several weeks.

Doctors, health centres and pharmacies

Public primary care in Spain is provided at a centro de salud, a health centre. Show your card and ask for public treatment, in Spanish “asistencia sanitaria pública”. The European Commission points out that hotels and tour operators may recommend private doctors, and that a request to pay upfront is a sign that the care is private.

Hospital treatment requires a referral from a doctor, except in an emergency. Prescriptions from public doctors can be filled at any pharmacy, a farmacia. As of November 2026 the patient charge for medicine is 50 per cent, and 10 per cent for pensioners. An ambulance is free when it is provided by the public health service. Keep every receipt if you end up paying yourself.

The words for a doctor’s visit are in the Spanish phrasebook, and a small first aid kit belongs on the Camino packing list.

112 and the AlertCops app

112 is the emergency number in Spain. The Policía Nacional also has the number 091, and the Guardia Civil has 062.

AlertCops is a free app from the Spanish Ministry of the Interior. It can be installed in eight languages, including English, and the chat offers translation in over 100 languages. You register with a phone number that can receive a text message, and no passport or ID is required.

FeatureWhat it does
ChatDirect contact with the Policía Nacional or the Guardia Civil
SOS buttonFive presses in a row send your location and 10 seconds of audio to your emergency contacts
Guardian Camino de SantiagoYou share your location with the security services while you walk one of the listed routes
Share locationYou share your position with up to five people you trust

Guardian Camino de Santiago is switched on under “Services” and “Guardian” in the app. According to the app’s own information the service covers, among others, the French, Portuguese and Primitive routes.

The Guardia Civil and the police on the route

The Guardia Civil has mobile offices for pilgrims, the Oficinas Móviles de Atención al Peregrino. In March 2025 the Galician regional government equipped them with five defibrillators. Since 2021 the Spanish Ministry of the Interior has run the campaign “No caminas sola” together with the Policía Nacional and the Guardia Civil. It is aimed in particular at women walking alone.

Heat and sun

The Spanish Ministry of Health runs a national heat plan, which in 2026 was active from 13 May to 30 September. The ministry and the weather service AEMET publish a heat risk level in four steps from green to red. The ministry’s advice includes:

  • Drink water often, even when you are not thirsty.
  • Avoid drinks with caffeine, alcohol or a lot of sugar.
  • Reduce physical activity during the hottest hours in the middle of the day.
  • Wear light, loose clothing that breathes.
  • See a health professional if symptoms that may be caused by the heat last more than an hour.

For a walker that means an early start and a long break in the shade in the middle of the day. The season makes a big difference, see the best time to walk the Camino.

Road sections and visibility

Some sections follow a road. According to the Spanish traffic authority DGT, pedestrians on a road must walk on the left, facing the oncoming traffic. Where there is a hard shoulder, you walk on it, and otherwise as close to the edge as possible. Several people walk in single file.

Between sunset and sunrise, and when the weather reduces visibility, pedestrians on the carriageway or the shoulder outside towns must carry an approved luminous or reflective element. The DGT also recommends light-coloured clothing.

Walking alone

In its leaflet for pilgrims from the Holy Year 2021 to 2022, the Policía Nacional advises walking in company and in daylight, especially from November to March. The leaflet also advises carrying a passport or ID, letting family and friends know where you are, keeping your phone charged and spreading your cash across several places.

This article gives no figures for crime on the Camino, because no official count was found. The practical side is covered in the guide to walking the Camino alone, and if you sleep in pilgrim hostels it is worth knowing about bed bugs on the Camino.

This article is general information and not medical or legal advice.

Frequently asked questions

Is the European Health Insurance Card enough for the Camino?
No. The card gives you medically necessary public healthcare in Spain on the same terms as people insured there, but it does not cover private healthcare, the cost of travelling home or lost and stolen property. The European Commission states that the card is not an alternative to travel insurance. Readers from outside the EU and the other participating countries have no card at all and need their own cover.
How do you get a European Health Insurance Card?
The card is free and is issued by your national health insurance provider, so the ordering process depends on the country where you are insured. Order it well before departure and check the expiry date if you already have one. It can be used in the 27 EU countries and in Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland and the United Kingdom, according to the European Commission.
What number do you call in an emergency in Spain?
Call 112, the emergency number in Spain. The Policía Nacional also has the number 091 and the Guardia Civil has 062. The AlertCops app from the Spanish Ministry of the Interior can be used alongside a call, because it sends your location and gives you a chat with the police, but treat it as a supplement to the call.
What is AlertCops and does it work for pilgrims?
AlertCops is a free app from the Spanish Ministry of the Interior. It has a chat with the Policía Nacional and the Guardia Civil, an SOS button and a service called Guardian Camino de Santiago, where you share your location with the security services while you walk. The app is available in eight languages, including English, and registration needs a phone number that can receive a text message.
What does medicine cost at a Spanish pharmacy with the card?
Prescriptions written by a doctor in the public system can be filled at any pharmacy. According to the European Commission's page on Spain, the patient pays 50 per cent of the price of the medicine, and pensioners from the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland pay 10 per cent. This was checked in November 2026. Medicine bought without a prescription is paid in full.
