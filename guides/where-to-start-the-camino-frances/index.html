Where to start the Camino Francés: ten towns compared

Sarria is the most used starting point on the Camino Francés, Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port the classic one. Compare distance, stages and 2025 figures.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

If you have about a week, start in Sarria. The town lies about 114 kilometres before Santiago, and that is enough to earn the Compostela. If you have five weeks, start in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port on the French side of the Pyrenees and walk the whole Camino Francés, about 770 kilometres.

Between those two extremes lie eight towns that many people use as a starting point: Roncesvalles, Pamplona, Logroño, Burgos, León, Astorga, Ponferrada and O Cebreiro. You are free to start wherever you like. The only formal rule concerns the Compostela, which requires at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot.

The distances are the sum of the stage lengths on the guide site Gronze, read on 20 November 2026. They follow the Napoleon route out of Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port, the historic route between León and Astorga and the path via San Xil between Triacastela and Sarria. Stages are Gronze’s own division, and for most people one stage equals one walking day. Other sources measure the route slightly differently, so you will come across other figures, including in the guide to how long the Camino is.

The pilgrim numbers come from the statistics of the pilgrim office in Santiago for the whole of 2025. They show how many of the pilgrims whose arrival was registered in Santiago gave the town as their starting point.

What the statistics show

The pilgrim office registered 530,844 pilgrims in 2025. Of those, 242,039 walked the Camino Francés, which is 45.67 per cent.

Sarria is in a league of its own. Its 161,901 pilgrims make up 32.43 per cent of all registered starting points on all routes. Set against the 242,039 on the Camino Francés, that is roughly two out of three.

Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port is second on the route with 30,343 pilgrims, 6.08 per cent of all starting points. León, O Cebreiro and Ponferrada follow. Logroño is the lowest of the ten with 536.

The figures have a limitation. They only count those who reach Santiago. If you walk for a week from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Logroño and go home, you are not in the statistics.

What each stretch is like

Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and Roncesvalles: the Pyrenees

The first stage from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Roncesvalles is 24.2 kilometres on the Napoleon route. The highest point is the Collado de Lepoeder at 1,430 metres, and Gronze rates the stage 5 for difficulty, the highest on the site. From 1 November to 31 March the lower route via Valcarlos is mandatory. It is 23.4 kilometres long.

If you start in Roncesvalles, you skip the mountain stage. Gronze writes that both Roncesvalles and Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port can be considered the start of the route today. The journey to both towns is covered in the guide to getting to the Camino.

Pamplona and Logroño: the first cities

Pamplona lies three stages after Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port and is the first larger city on the route. From here there are 30 stages to Santiago. Logroño is four stages further on, with 26 stages left. From both cities you get most of the route, but without the mountain stage over the Pyrenees.

Burgos and León: the Meseta

The Meseta begins on the way out of Burgos. Gronze describes long gravel tracks through the cereal fields of Castile, few places with shade, a burning sun in summer and severe cold in winter. From Burgos to León there are eight stages and about 178 kilometres.

If you start in León, you get the last two stages of the plain. At Astorga, according to Gronze, the route leaves the great plain behind for good.

Astorga and Ponferrada: the mountains of León

From Astorga the route climbs into the Montes de León through the Maragatería district. The ascent is gentle as far as Rabanal and then becomes steeper. Ponferrada lies on the other side of the mountains, about 204 kilometres and nine stages from Santiago.

O Cebreiro and Sarria: Galicia

Gronze calls the stage up to O Cebreiro the biggest climb on the Camino Francés on Spanish soil: 660 metres of ascent over about 12 kilometres. The village is in Galicia. If you start here, you begin at the top.

From Sarria the route runs through rural Galicia, between meadows and woods of oak and chestnut and past countless small hamlets. Gronze describes the first stage as free of particular difficulties, with frequent but moderate gradients. Read more about the last 100 km from Sarria.

The 100 km rule for the Compostela

The Compostela is the certificate issued by the pilgrim office in Santiago. The office’s requirements are:

  • At least 100 continuous kilometres on foot or on horseback on the same route to Santiago.
  • At least 200 kilometres if you cycle.
  • The final stage into the cathedral in Santiago has to be included.
  • You may walk the route in several trips, as long as the stages follow each other in time and geography.
  • If you only walk the minimum distance, you need a dated stamp at the start and end of every stage.

All ten towns in the table are more than 100 kilometres from Santiago. Sarria is the last of them, which explains a large part of its popularity. You collect the stamps in your pilgrim passport, the credencial. The Camino glossary covers the vocabulary.

Frequently asked questions

Where do most people start the Camino Francés?
In Sarria. In the pilgrim office statistics for 2025, 161,901 pilgrims gave Sarria as their starting point, which is 32.43 per cent of all registered starting points on all routes. Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port was the second most used start on the Camino Francés with 30,343 pilgrims. León, O Cebreiro and Ponferrada came next.
How far is it from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Santiago?
Adding up the stage lengths published by the guide site Gronze gives about 764 kilometres from Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port to Santiago de Compostela, split into 33 stages. That figure follows the Napoleon route over the Pyrenees, the historic route between León and Astorga and the path via San Xil. Other sources measure slightly differently and arrive at other totals.
Can you get the Compostela if you start in Sarria?
Yes. The pilgrim office in Santiago asks for at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot on the same route, and the final stage into the cathedral has to be included. From Sarria it is about 114 kilometres by Gronze's stage lengths. If you only walk the minimum distance, you need a dated stamp at the start and end of every stage.
Should you start in Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port or Roncesvalles?
According to Gronze, both can be considered the start of the Camino Francés today. From Saint-Jean-Pied-de-Port you get the stage over the Pyrenees, which Gronze gives its highest difficulty rating. From Roncesvalles you skip it and have about 740 kilometres to Santiago. From 1 November to 31 March the route via Valcarlos is mandatory.
How many days do you need from León to Santiago?
Gronze divides the stretch from León to Santiago into 13 stages covering about 305 kilometres in total. At one stage a day that is 13 walking days. Add a day or two if you want a rest day along the way, and remember the travel days at each end. The stages are between 17.8 and 28.5 kilometres long.
