Luggage transfer on the Camino: how it works

How luggage transfer works on the Camino. Booking, labels, weight limits, prices from Correos and private carriers, and what it means for the Compostela.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Luggage transfer on the Camino works like this: you book with a carrier, put a label on your bag and leave it at reception in the morning. The carrier drives it to your next accommodation, where it is waiting when you arrive. You walk with a small daypack holding water, rain gear and valuables.

The service exists on all the main routes in season, and it does not cost you the right to a Compostela. It has two practical limits. You need to know your next accommodation by the evening before, and many public albergues do not accept transported bags.

How it works day by day

The Spanish postal service Correos describes its Paq Mochila service in the most detail, and the private carriers follow the same pattern. The figures below are Correos’ own, checked in November 2026.

StepRule at Correos
BookingOnline, by 8 pm the day before the transfer
LabellingLabel sent by email, or a piece of paper with your details
Drop-offAt reception before 8 am
DeliveryAt the next accommodation before 2.30 pm
WeightAt most 15 kg per bag
DistanceAt most 60 km per day

If you have not booked online, you use a Paq Mochila envelope, which many accommodations keep. Everything has to be packed inside the bag. Boots, sleeping bags and mats must not hang on the outside.

The accommodation needs a reception that is open from 8 am to 3 pm, or an access code for the door. You need a reservation or the accommodation’s clear permission to leave the bag there.

Correos does not operate on 24, 25 and 31 December or 1 January. The guide site Gronze keeps a full list of several smaller carriers, including on the Camino Portugués and the Camino Inglés. The Camino Francés gives you the widest choice of carriers.

What it costs

None of the four carriers shows a fixed price per stage on its front page. The price is calculated in the booking form from the route and the number of stages. These are the figures that could be confirmed with the carriers themselves:

  • Correos stated in March 2026 that Sarria to Santiago cost 30 euros in total for one bag of up to 15 kg, however many days you spend on the stretch.
  • Correos gives a discount when you book five stages or more. The size of the discount is not stated.
  • Jacotrans has a limit of 15 kg per item and gives quotes by email based on dates, towns and number of bags.

The travel agency Santiago Ways gives a typical level of 4 to 7 euros per stage and a usual weight limit of 20 kg. The figure is undated and does not come from a carrier, so use it only as a rough guide.

Public albergues rarely accept bags

Correos writes plainly that it is not possible to leave backpacks in the public albergues. If you stay in one, you must arrange another collection point, for example a bar or a private albergue in the same town, and tell Correos.

This follows from the albergues’ rules. In Galicia, the Xunta’s Decree 45/2001 states that places in the public albergues are allocated on arrival and that reservations are not possible. The decree puts people travelling with a support car last in the queue, after pilgrims on foot, on horseback and by bicycle.

If you want to use luggage transfer every day, you should therefore plan on private albergues, guesthouses or hotels. Look up the Spanish accommodation terms in the Camino glossary before you book.

Send a suitcase straight to Santiago

If you bring clothes for a holiday after the walk, you can send them ahead. Correos’ Paq Peregrino service costs from 20.95 euros (checked November 2026) and is offered for parcels of up to 15 kg and up to 25 kg. The price covers 15 days of storage, and you can buy an extension in advance. The parcel can be sent to Santiago, to a post office along the route or to a private address.

The Correos post office in Santiago is at Rúa do Franco 4, close to the cathedral, and also has left luggage. Jacotrans and Caminofácil offer shipping and storage in Santiago as well. See also the guide to arriving in Santiago.

Does it affect your Compostela

No. The pilgrim office in Santiago requires you to cover at least 100 continuous kilometres on the same route on foot or on horseback, with the final stage leading into Santiago. The credencial must be stamped along the way, and the office asks for at least two stamps a day over the last 100 kilometres.

The rules mention neither backpacks nor luggage transfer. They exclude other ways of travelling than on foot, on horseback and by bicycle, except for people with disabilities. So you are the one who has to walk the distance. The bag is allowed to ride. The guide to the last 100 km from Sarria covers the shortest stretch that qualifies.

Is it right for you

Luggage transfer makes sense if your back, knees or hips cannot take the weight, if you are bringing a lot, or if you are walking a short trip with fixed accommodation. It suits you less if you like to decide the day’s end point along the way or want to sleep in public albergues. A lightly packed backpack is the alternative that gives you full freedom.

Frequently asked questions

How much does luggage transfer cost on the Camino?
It depends on the route, the carrier and the number of stages. Correos stated in March 2026 that the stretch from Sarria to Santiago cost 30 euros in total for one bag of up to 15 kg, with a discount from five stages. The exact price only appears in the booking form. The private carriers do not show a fixed stage price on their front pages either, so ask for a quote before you book.
Can you send your backpack to a public albergue?
Usually not. Correos writes that it is not possible to leave backpacks in the public albergues and that you must arrange another collection point instead. The public albergues in Galicia do not take reservations, and the carriers normally require a reservation or a clear agreement with the accommodation. Private albergues, guesthouses and hotels with a reception are the safe choice.
Do you lose the right to a Compostela if you use luggage transfer?
The pilgrim office's rules are about how you travel yourself. You must walk at least 100 continuous kilometres on the same route, collect stamps in your credencial and finish in Santiago. The rules do not mention the backpack and do not require you to carry it. Luggage transfer is therefore no obstacle, as long as you walk the whole distance yourself.
How heavy can the bag be?
Correos sets the limit at 15 kg per bag, and Jacotrans states the same limit of 15 kg per item. With Correos, everything has to be packed inside the bag. Boots, sleeping bags or mats must not hang on the outside. If you bring a larger suitcase, it is wiser to send it straight to Santiago and let only a smaller bag follow you from stage to stage.
When do you have to book?
With Correos, the booking must be made by 8 pm the day before the transfer, and you can change the coming stages before the same deadline. Only the first night's accommodation has to be given when you book. That leaves room to decide where to sleep along the way, but you need to know where you will sleep the next night by the evening before.
Does luggage transfer run all year?
Not on every route. In 2026 Correos ran its full network from 16 March to 31 October. From 1 November 2026 the service only covers Sarria to Santiago on the Camino Francés and Tui to Santiago on the Camino Portugués. NCS stated in November 2026 that its season had ended. Jacotrans writes that it operates all year. Always check the dates if you walk outside the season.
