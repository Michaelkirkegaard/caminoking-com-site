The Camino in comfort: hotels, Paradores and packages

The Camino can be walked without dormitories. See the options from private room to Parador, booking, luggage transfer, packages and the Compostela rules.

Camino King · 21 November 2026 · 8 min read

The Camino does not have to mean dormitories. Along the routes there are private albergues with private rooms, pensiones, hostales, casas rurales, hotels and the state-owned Paradores, and you can have your luggage driven from bed to bed.

The Compostela does not depend on where you sleep. The Pilgrim’s Reception Office in Santiago requires at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot on the same route and a stamped pilgrim passport, and the office itself lists hotels among the places where you can collect stamps.

Accommodation beyond the dormitory

The public albergue is the cheapest step. In Galicia a night in the Xunta’s public hostels costs 10 euros per person, and according to the Xunta the network has 76 hostels with more than 3,000 places. From there both price and privacy go up.

In Tui on the Camino Portugués the same listings showed dormitory beds at 16 to 18 euros and double rooms at 40 to 66 euros. Gronze shows the price per bed for dormitories and per room for private rooms, so two people sharing a double room split the amount. What a whole walk adds up to is covered in the guide to how much the Camino costs.

Paradores along the routes

Paradores is a state-owned hotel chain. According to Wikipedia’s article on the chain, the first Parador opened in 1928, and many of the hotels are in castles, palaces, fortresses and monasteries. Several stand directly on the Camino, and some of the buildings once received pilgrims themselves.

  • Santo Domingo de la Calzada. Opposite the cathedral stands the old Pilgrims’ Hospital, which is a Parador today. Spain’s official tourism portal writes that the original 12th-century building was put up by Santo Domingo to take in pilgrims.
  • León. The tourism portal describes the convent of San Marcos as a convent hospital that was used as a hospital for pilgrims on the road to Santiago. Today it is a Parador.
  • Santiago de Compostela. According to the tourism portal, the Hostal dos Reis Católicos on Praza do Obradoiro was founded in 1499 by the Catholic Monarchs as a hostel for pilgrims who had completed the Way. The Pilgrim’s Reception Office writes that the hotel still offers free meals to the first pilgrims who turn up each day.
  • Camino Portugués. The Xunta de Galicia mentions that the Monterreal fortress in Baiona on the coastal route is a Parador today. Wikipedia’s list also has Paradores in Tui and Pontevedra.

The sources give no room prices for Paradores, so this article does not state any.

What booking ahead means

The public hostels in Galicia do not take reservations. The Xunta writes that places are occupied as pilgrims arrive and that in no case is it possible to make reservations. The doors open at 1 p.m., you stay one night, and pilgrims with a support car are last in line.

On the other steps of the ladder it works the other way round. You book a private room in advance, and the day’s destination is then fixed. Gronze writes about luggage transfer that the service requires stages and accommodation to be planned beforehand. That secures a bed, but you can no longer stop early or walk on if the day turns out differently from what you expected. On the busiest stretches, such as the last 100 km from Sarria, many pilgrims are on the move. From Sarria alone the Pilgrim’s Reception Office registered 161,921 pilgrims in 2025.

Luggage transfer

You can have a backpack or suitcase driven from one night’s accommodation to the next. Gronze describes the service this way: you leave the bag ready in the morning and find it at the next place when the stage is over. The Spanish postal service Correos requires booking by 8 p.m. the day before, a maximum of 15 kilos per item, and a reservation or permission from the place where the bag is delivered. Prices, companies and rules are gathered in the guide to luggage transfer on the Camino.

Shorter stages

The Pilgrim’s Reception Office states that people cover an average of 25 kilometres a day on foot and that the pace varies. The stages in a guidebook are suggestions. The stretch from Tui to Santiago is an example: Gronze divides it into five stages with a first day of 31.9 kilometres, while the Xunta de Galicia divides its 118.8 kilometres into six stages and so comes down to just under 20 kilometres on average.

If you walk only the minimum distance, the Pilgrim’s Reception Office requires the pilgrim passport to be stamped with the date at the start and end of each stage. Short days therefore leave your right to the Compostela unchanged as long as the kilometres are continuous. Which route suits short days is covered in the guide to choosing a Camino route.

Packages from tour operators

Tour operators sell both group trips with a tour leader and trips where you walk on your own with everything booked. This article recommends no operator and gives no prices, because they depend on route, season and standard.

The guide site Albergues Camino Santiago describes an organised trip like this: the agency selects and books private albergues, hostales and pensiones, moves the luggage to the next stop and helps choose the route and the stages. On group trips a tour leader walks along, and there is a support vehicle if someone cannot continue. The article on that site contains an affiliate link to an agency.

One operator’s description of a self-guided trip, reviewed for this article, mentions these parts:

  • en suite rooms in hotels and guest houses of around three stars
  • breakfast every day, and dinner in the small towns where choice is limited
  • luggage transfer on every walking day, one bag per person of up to 20 kilos
  • walking notes and maps
  • a phone number answered around the clock

Lunch is left out, and the page mentions neither flights, insurance nor the pilgrim passport. Ask about those three before you book.

Compostela, pilgrim passport and stamps in hotels

The Pilgrim’s Reception Office’s conditions for the Compostela do not mention accommodation. In short they are:

  1. At least 100 continuous kilometres on foot or on horseback on the same recognised route, or 200 kilometres by bicycle.
  2. The final stage must be the one that leads into the cathedral in Santiago.
  3. The pilgrim passport must be stamped along the way, and on the last 100 kilometres on foot with at least two stamps a day.

According to the Pilgrim’s Reception Office, stamps are normally collected where pilgrims sleep, and they can also be obtained in parish churches, monasteries, cathedrals, hotels, town halls and other places. The pilgrim passport is required if you want to use the pilgrim hostels. The rules are therefore the same whether you sleep in a dormitory or in a Parador. The whole process from passport to certificate is described in the guide to the pilgrim passport and the Compostela, and the overview of the Way itself is on the page about the Camino de Santiago.

Frequently asked questions

Can you walk the Camino without staying in albergues?
Yes. The Pilgrim's Reception Office in Santiago sets no requirement about where you sleep. Along the routes there are private albergues with private rooms, pensiones, hostales, casas rurales, hotels and Paradores. Listings on Gronze show, for example, single rooms from 30 euros in Sarria. The pilgrim passport is only required if you want to use the pilgrim hostels.
Do you get the Compostela if you stay in hotels?
Yes. The Pilgrim's Reception Office's conditions are about the walk and say nothing about the bed: at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot or on horseback on the same recognised route, a final stage that leads into the cathedral in Santiago, and a pilgrim passport stamped along the way. On the last 100 kilometres you need at least two stamps a day.
Can you get pilgrim passport stamps in hotels?
Yes. The Pilgrim's Reception Office writes that stamps are normally collected where pilgrims sleep, such as hostels, and that they can also be obtained in parish churches, monasteries, cathedrals, hotels, town halls and other places. On the last 100 kilometres on foot you need at least two stamps a day.
What is a Parador?
Paradores is a state-owned Spanish hotel chain in which many of the hotels occupy historic buildings such as castles, palaces, monasteries and fortresses, according to Wikipedia's article on the chain. On the Camino Francés there are Paradores in Santo Domingo de la Calzada, León and Santiago de Compostela, among others, and Spain's official tourism portal describes all three buildings as former hospitals or hostels for pilgrims.
What does a Camino tour package usually include?
A tour operator chooses and books the accommodation and moves your luggage from place to place. One operator whose package was reviewed for this article describes en suite rooms in hotels and guest houses of around three stars, breakfast every day, transfer of one bag of up to 20 kilos, walking notes and a phone number answered around the clock. Lunch is left out. The contents vary, so read each operator's own list.
Do you need to book accommodation ahead on the Camino?
It depends on the type. The public hostels in Galicia cannot be reserved, and beds go to whoever arrives first. Private rooms, pensiones and hotels can be reserved. If you use luggage transfer, Correos requires that you hold a reservation or have permission from the place where the bag will be left.
