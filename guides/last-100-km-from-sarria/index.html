The last 100 km from Sarria: the Camino in a week

Sarria to Santiago is about 114 km in five stages. See the stages, the Compostela rules, how busy it gets and five quieter routes of the same length.

Camino King · 20 November 2026 · 6 min read

  2. The Compostela rules for the last 100 km

  5. The same length on other routes

Sarria to Santiago de Compostela is about 114 km, and most people walk it in five or six days. Add a travel day at each end and it fits a one-week holiday. The stretch is long enough for a Compostela, because the pilgrim office asks for at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot on the same route.

It is also the busiest start on the Camino. In 2025, 162,038 pilgrims began in Sarria, roughly three in ten of everyone who arrived in Santiago that year. If you want more quiet, five other routes have stretches of the same length. They are listed at the end of this article.

The stages from Sarria to Santiago

The table follows the guide site Gronze’s breakdown of the Camino Francés. Other guides measure slightly differently, so expect small variations.

DayStageDistance
1Sarria to Portomarín22.2 km
2Portomarín to Palas de Rei24.8 km
3Palas de Rei to Arzúa28.5 km
4Arzúa to O Pedrouzo19.3 km
5O Pedrouzo to Santiago de Compostela19.4 km
Total114.2 km

Gronze describes the first stage as having no notable difficulties, with frequent but moderate climbs and descents.

Five or six days

Five days is the usual plan. Day 3 is the longest at 28.5 km, and the town of Melide lies on that very stage. Split the day there and the walk becomes six days with shorter stages. For the wider picture of distances, see how long the Camino is.

The Compostela rules for the last 100 km

The Compostela is the cathedral’s certificate that you have completed the pilgrimage. The pilgrim office in Santiago sets these conditions, checked on 20 November 2026:

  • You must walk at least 100 continuous kilometres on the same route. By bicycle the requirement is 200 km.
  • The final stage, the one that ends at the cathedral, must be included.
  • You need at least two stamps a day for at least the last 100 km if you are walking.
  • If you only do the minimum distance, you must always get a stamp at the starting point.
  • You may walk the route in sections over several trips, as long as the sections are in chronological and geographical order.

You collect the stamps in your pilgrim passport, the credencial. According to the pilgrim office you usually get them where you sleep, but also in parish churches, monasteries, cathedrals, hotels and town halls. A simple habit is one stamp along the way and one when you arrive.

The Spanish federation of Camino associations gives Barbadelo as the 100 km point on the Camino Francés. With 114.2 km from Sarria, you have a little to spare.

How busy is it?

Very. These figures are the pilgrim office’s count for 2025, as reported by Correos on 8 January 2026:

  • 530,987 pilgrims arrived in Santiago in 2025.
  • 162,038 of them started in Sarria, the most used starting point of all.
  • 242,175 walked the Camino Francés, or 46 per cent.
  • September was the busiest month, followed by August and May.

That means many people on the path and pressure on beds in high season. How busy it feels on a given day varies with the month and the day of the week. The guide to the best time to walk the Camino covers the seasons.

How to reach Sarria

Sarria has a railway station. According to Gronze there are direct trains, or trains with a change at Monforte de Lemos, from Madrid, Barcelona and Bilbao, and night trains from Madrid and Barcelona too. Timetables are on Renfe’s site.

The nearest airport is Santiago de Compostela. From the airport Monbus runs to Lugo five to seven times a day in about two hours. From Lugo a bus leaves for Sarria roughly once an hour, and the ride takes about 35 minutes. Timetables change, so check them with the operators before you book flights. The wider journey is covered in the guide to getting to the Camino.

The same length on other routes

The 100 km rule applies on all the recognised routes, so you can walk a one-week Camino from places other than Sarria. The distances are added up from Gronze’s stages.

A few things are worth knowing:

  • Tui was the starting point for 41,646 pilgrims in 2025 and Ferrol for 28,885. That is far fewer than Sarria.
  • Lugo: the Xunta de Galicia placed a 100 km marker stone in the city in 2022, and the federation of associations gives Lugo as the 100 km point. In Melide the route merges with the Camino Francés.
  • Ourense: by Canedo the first stage is slightly shorter, and the walk comes to 104.3 km.
  • Baamonde sits close to the limit. The stage to Sobrado dos Monxes is 39.8 km by Miraz but only 32.0 km by As Cruces, and then the total by Gronze’s figures is just 92.7 km. If you choose the short variant, start in Vilalba.
  • A Coruña on the Camino Inglés is too short by itself. By Gronze’s stages it is 72.4 km to Santiago.

If you have not chosen a route yet, compare them in the guide to which Camino route. And when the goal is near, read what happens when arriving in Santiago.

Frequently asked questions

How many days does it take to walk from Sarria to Santiago?
Most people take five walking days, because the usual breakdown has five stages of between 19.3 and 28.5 km, 114.2 km in total by Gronze's figures. The longest stage runs from Palas de Rei to Arzúa and can be split in Melide, which makes it a six-day walk. With a travel day at each end, that fits a one-week holiday.
Can you get a Compostela if you only walk from Sarria?
Yes. The pilgrim office in Santiago asks for at least 100 continuous kilometres on foot on the same route, and the final stage must end at the cathedral. Sarria is about 114 km out. You need a pilgrim passport with at least two stamps a day over the last 100 km, and you also need a stamp from the place where you start.
How many stamps a day do you need from Sarria?
The pilgrim office says it is essential to have at least two stamps a day for at least the last 100 km if you are walking. The stamps must be dated. You usually get them where you sleep, but also in parish churches, monasteries, cathedrals, hotels and town halls. Collect one in the morning or along the way and one when you arrive.
How busy is the stretch from Sarria?
Sarria is the most used starting point on the whole Camino. In 2025, 162,038 pilgrims began here out of the 530,987 who arrived in Santiago, roughly three in ten. September was the busiest month in 2025, followed by August and May. Outside those months there are fewer people on the path, but the stretch is never empty.
How do you get to Sarria?
Sarria has a railway station with direct trains, or trains with a change at Monforte de Lemos, from Madrid, Barcelona and Bilbao. The nearest airport is Santiago de Compostela. From there Monbus runs to Lugo in about two hours, and from Lugo a bus leaves for Sarria roughly once an hour and takes about 35 minutes. Always check current times with Renfe and Monbus.
Are there other routes with a last 100 km?
Yes. You can walk from Tui on the Camino Portugués, from Ferrol on the Camino Inglés, from Lugo on the Camino Primitivo, from Ourense on the Camino Sanabrés and from Vilalba or Baamonde on the Camino del Norte. All of them lie around 100 to 120 km from Santiago, and all had far fewer pilgrims than Sarria in 2025.
